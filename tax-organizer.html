A tax client portal built compliance-first.

I designed and delivered a secure client portal for a boutique tax-preparation firm. Clients get a personal document checklist, upload forms from their phone, and let AI pre-fill their organizer. Every screen was shaped by the FTC Safeguards Rule, IRS Publication 4557 and IRC §7216, then enforced in the database, not just the interface.

Lessons learned

What I'd tell any team building software that handles taxpayer data.

Security friction is a business decision, so make it visible

Mandatory authenticator-app MFA is the strongest option, but it can scare off less technical clients. I kept it for launch and laid out the email/text-code trade-off for the owner to decide, instead of quietly weakening the control.

Least privilege usually arrives in version two, so plan it in version one

"Every preparer sees every client" was quick to build and wrong for a growing firm. I moved to explicit, audited assignments and a separate Admin role.

Protect the keys to the kingdom

Role changes are serialized, audited, and can never remove the last administrator. One mistake shouldn't lock a firm out of its own data.

Logs and error messages can leak, too

Free-text notes and error telemetry can capture SSNs. Masking at the database and generic error messages closed that gap.

AI should assist, and people should confirm

Extraction highlights low-confidence fields and duplicates, and the client confirms every value. The §7216 consent discloses that AI reads documents only to fill in the organizer.

Retention is an operation, not a setting

A purge job needs scheduling, authentication, batching and per-file auditing, and it stays paused until the app is live.

Technical controls don't equal compliance

The software supports the Safeguards Rule, but the firm still owns its WISP, Qualified Individual, risk assessment, training, vendor oversight and incident response. I documented that boundary explicitly.
